# FarmARE L3 Soybean Formal Scenario System

## 1. Overview

The FarmARE formal evaluation pool contains 90 L3 full-season soybean scenarios. The pool covers six real soybean seed types: HEIHE43, HEIHE50, HEINONG58, HEINONG60, HEINONG84, and HEIKE71. An L3 full-season soybean scenario starts at pre-planting or planting-window management and continues through emergence, in-season water/nutrient/biotic management, R5/R6, maturity, harvest, drying, and storage. The final formal pool evaluates whether an agent can follow evidence-driven expert farm management under cultivar, weather, soil, biotic, resource, capacity, and postharvest-quality constraints.

## 2. Scenario Script Architecture

L3 full-season scenarios live primarily under `are/simulation/scenarios/scenario_farm_world_fullseason_v2/`. A scenario wrapper file normally inherits from `Scenario`; in `init_and_populate_apps()` it reads a catalog `SPEC`, calls `init_batch_apps()` to install FarmWorld, Weather, Sensor, Drone, Robot, Tractor, FieldOps, and related apps, and then calls `build_batch_events()` to construct the oracle event graph. `harbin_l3_batch_catalog.py` stores catalog-driven/class-config `ScenarioSpec` objects, including `scenario_id`, `profile_name`, `briefing_text`, seed stocks, planting zones, soil hydraulic modifiers, prior histories, management regimes, postharvest market settings, action windows, and harvest zones. `profiles.py` supplies physics profiles and real seed parameters, linking hidden agronomic conditions to the physics engines.

The codebase uses several scenario forms. Standalone ARE-style scenario scripts write the scenario class and event sequence directly. Catalog-driven/class-config scenarios use a small wrapper plus a reusable `ScenarioSpec`. Trace runners under `scripts/fullseason/` are diagnostic wrappers that insert a trace-only app; they are not production scenario definitions. Oracle flow construction is handled by functions such as `build_batch_events()`, `_prep_field()`, `_plant_zone()`, `_wait_check_window()`, `_diagnose_target()`, `_run_window_actions()`, and `harvest_range()`. Evidence/export artifacts are generated from the real event log, daily state snapshots, and final summaries.

During setup, `__init__` and scenario config establish the scenario id, start time, apps, inventory, management regime, and postharvest market settings. Profile setup defines hidden weather, soil, residue, drainage, biotic, resource, capacity, and seed-response conditions. Seed plans and ridge zones are assigned through `PlantingZone`, `zones`, `harvest_zones`, and soil/history modifiers. The oracle sends an agent-facing briefing, performs weather/soil/inventory/machine checks, prepares and plants the field, then maps observations from sensors, drones, robots, and range state into farm operations: planting, emergence/stand checks, fertigation, irrigation, weed control, fungicide, pesticide, harvest, drying, storage, and capacity or budget handling.

## 3. Evidence Pipeline

Trace runners under `scripts/fullseason/` use `make_trace_scenario()` to insert `HarbinL3DailyTraceApp` into a production scenario. Its `capture_daily_state()` method reads FarmWorld physics state, the weather snapshot, management regime, postharvest market settings, zone summaries, ridge details, and recent action log at key points. The trace utility then writes field summary CSV, ridge-state CSV, and oracle trace JSON.

The final acceptance artifacts organize this evidence into `raw_event_log`, `daily_state`, `checkpoint_summary`, `final_summary`, `compact_action_workflows`, `representative_ridge_yield`, the final one-row scenario inventory, and the final acceptance table. `raw_event_log` preserves true event order. `daily_state` shows ridge or zone stage, GDD, NDVI, LAI, biomass, soil moisture, water/nutrient/biotic pressures, grain moisture, and yield proxy values. `checkpoint_summary` extracts planting, emergence, stress onset, intervention, R5/R6, R8, harvest, and storage checkpoints. `compact_action_workflows` compresses repeated tool calls into human-readable farm-operation steps. `representative_ridge_yield` summarizes affected, reference, seed, density, or harvest-batch zones. The final inventory and acceptance table support human review of agent task text, seed plan, oracle workflow, primary metric, and final yield evidence for all 90 formal scenarios.

## 4. Ten Scenario Types

- Type 0: Basic ICMLW / baseline scenarios: basic full-season soybean management, standard tasks, standard oracle flow, and existing ICMLW scenario lineage.
- Type 1: Soybean seed variants: real cultivars HEIHE43, HEIHE50, HEINONG58, HEINONG60, HEINONG84, and HEIKE71; differences include maturity, emergence, density optimum, stress tolerance, resistance, and yield potential.
- Type 2: Staggered plot cycles: ridge blocks planted on different dates or managed at different crop stages, producing different emergence, GDD, phenology, grain moisture, and harvest windows.
- Type 3: Season weather types: dry season, wet June, cold spring, hot/dry summer, cool August, and late-rain season; weather changes correct oracle timing.
- Type 4: Soil water-holding and drainage variants: fast-draining soil, poor drainage, compaction, low water holding, residue/trafficability, and local soil constraints.
- Type 5: Biotic-pressure regimes: pest, weed, and disease pressures, including diagnostic confusion such as low NDVI from disease, weeds, or insects.
- Type 6: Management objective variants: maximize yield, minimize water, minimize chemical input, minimize machinery passes, or protect harvest quality.
- Type 7: Operational capacity and timing constraints: spray budget, irrigation quota, fertilizer quota, harvester days, drying capacity, storage capacity, machinery availability, and fuel limits.
- Type 8: Rotation and prior-field-history regimes: soy-after-soy, high residue, compacted field, weed seedbank, prior disease history, and nutrient carryover.
- Type 9: Market and post-harvest quality regimes: grain moisture, drying cost, storage safety, market discount, quality batch, high-moisture delivery, and harvest timing.
- Type 10: Sustainability and organic-management regimes: organic, low chemical, low input, water conservation, low carbon, and allowed inputs.

## 6. Yield and Primary Metric Impact Sanity

Wrong agent behavior does not always need to create a large final-yield drop. Water/drought, disease, insect, weed, nutrient/fertility, establishment/stand, and cultivar/stage-management scenarios should usually show effects in final yield, representative-zone biomass, NDVI, LAI, or stress state. Postharvest, sensor, market/quality, low-carbon/fuel, and resource-budget scenarios may be evaluated mainly by their primary metrics, such as dryer capacity, storage status, sensor_status, alternative_observation_used, discount_loss, operation_count, fuel_proxy, water_budget_remaining, or spray_budget_remaining.

| scenario group | wrong policy example | expected signal | evidence field |
|---|---|---|---|
| water/drought | irrigate the whole field or miss the real dry patch | low target-zone water_stress, poor budget use, lower yield or biomass | soil_moisture, water_stress, irrigated_ridges, water_budget_remaining, final_yield_kg |
| disease | spray whole field without confirmation, or fail to treat after evidence is strong | disease_pressure remains high or target is wrong | disease_pressure, fungicide_target, post_treatment_trend, final_yield_kg |
| insect | spray below evidence threshold or ignore damaging insect pressure | insect_pressure and spray budget behave poorly | insect_pressure, pesticide_target, spray_budget_remaining, yield proxy |
| weed | read high field NDVI as healthy crop growth | weed_pressure or weed_cover remains high and crop state recovers poorly | weed_pressure, weed_cover, field_ndvi, crop_ndvi, LAI, biomass |
| nutrient/fertility | treat disease or water stress as nutrient deficiency, or miss the true nutrient zone | nutrient_stress and biomass recovery remain weak | nutrient_stress, fertigation_target, biomass, NDVI, final_yield_kg |
| establishment/stand | miss a skip-row or weak-stand recovery window, or replant whole field | stand_fraction, LAI, and maturity window are affected | stand_fraction, emergence check, replant_target, stage, final_yield_kg |
| cultivar/stage management | treat all cultivars or planting dates as the same crop state | zone harvest, maturity, water, or disease decisions are wrong | growth_stage_by_zone, GDD, grain_moisture, treatment_target_by_zone |
| postharvest | store wet grain directly or ignore batch capacity | drying or storage state is unsafe | grain_moisture, drying_status, storage_status, storage_grain_moisture |
| sensor/quality/market | fail to use alternative observation, or ignore a moisture discount | primary metric degrades even if biological yield is similar | sensor_status, alternative_observation_used, moisture_discount, discount_loss, net_return_proxy |
| low-carbon/fuel/resource budget | waste scarce resources before a more important window | resource remaining and operation proxy become worse | operation_count, fuel_proxy, machine_available_days, budget_remaining |
