## Drought water-balance candidate

`drought_water_balance_v5` retains v4 weather, soil parameters, treatment date,
target ridges 20–43, harvest calendar, native acceptance, yield/reward functions,
and screening criteria (50% stressed, 1% marketable omission loss in every pair).
It changes two explicitly coupled mechanisms:

1. Subdaily irrigation conserves effective input: infiltration-cap excess becomes
   recorded runoff, topsoil saturation overflow moves into roots, and root
   saturation overflow becomes recorded drainage. This follows the existing
   daily soil engine's overflow accounting. Older variants retain legacy behavior.
2. The single native irrigation pulse is 60 mm (12 hours at native 5 mm/hour),
   with field-equivalent quota 60 × 24/64 = 22.5 mm. This is an author-defined
   modeling choice, not a site-calibrated irrigation recommendation.

The pulse is derived **before new yield measurements** from saved pre-intervention
states of v4 development worlds 0–9 only. Desired immediate root VWC is native
stress threshold + 0.02 m³/m³; that buffer is an explicit modeling assumption.
For each target ridge, calculate the water needed to reach that root storage
plus topsoil retained after native gravity redistribution, divided by native
irrigation efficiency. Maximum per-world requirements in mm are 58.447, 54.283,
51.844, 54.552, 54.859, 54.088, 57.626, 58.398, 55.880, 55.976. Round the maximum
up to the next 5 mm: **60 mm**. No yield response enters this calculation.
Run this one candidate on all ten development pairs; retain every outcome.
Do not iterate doses against held-out yield losses.

Published support establishes stage-dependent soybean water sensitivity and
the importance of soil storage; it does not validate the numerical simulator
parameters: [FAO, Kirda, deficit irrigation scheduling](https://www.fao.org/4/Y3655E/y3655e03.htm).
